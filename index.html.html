Day-Before Study Guide: Seminar on Green Energy for Indonesia (BIOMA, 10–23 October 2026)

Built from the official schedule (Session 227). Each day has one "Read the evening before" block (target 1–2 hours) for the next day's activities: key points, terminology, comparison with Indonesia and the Ministry of Energy and Mineral Resources (MoEMR/ESDM), and discussion questions to raise in each session.

Accuracy note: figures are approximate, from general knowledge. Always cross-check against the lecturers' materials and the latest official MoEMR data (e.g., Handbook of Energy & Economic Statistics, RUPTL, ministerial regulations). If the lecturer's number differs, record theirs with the source.

0. General Strategy (read once, apply daily)

Daily routine

  1. Evening before (60–120 min): read the next day's block, write 3 questions of your own, prepare one "comparison with Indonesia".
  2. During class: take notes in 3 columns: Facts/figures | Mechanism/technology | Relevance to Indonesia/MoEMR.
  3. After class (15 min): write a 5-line summary and 1 idea applicable to your unit/region.
  4. Photograph slides (if permitted) and collect lecturers' contacts.

One-page note template: Topic & speaker · 3 key points · Key figures (capacity, cost, efficiency, price) · Policies/regulations mentioned · What is applicable in Indonesia and what are the barriers? · Unanswered questions.

How to ask questions like an expert

  • Start with context: "In Indonesia we face X. How did you address it in China?"
  • Ask for numbers: cost per kWp / per m³ biogas / per tonne of waste, payback, tariffs, subsidies.
  • Ask about failures and lessons learned: "What did not work, and why?"
  • Ask about business model & institutions: who finances, who operates, how long-term maintenance is ensured.

Practical notes

  • Halal: request halal food (清真 qīngzhēn) from organizers early, especially for banquets and field visits; ask about prayer space/time.
  • Payments/apps: Alipay/WeChat Pay can link to international cards; carry some cash. Google and many Western apps do not work normally without an eSIM/international roaming.
  • China emergency numbers: police 110, ambulance 120, fire 119, traffic 122.
  • Bring: passport (always), jacket (Chengdu is damp and cool in October; Kunming is cool), comfortable field shoes, power bank, adapter, personal medication, business cards.
  • Schedule note: the schedule title says up to 23 Oct, but a 24 Oct departure transfer is listed. Confirm your return ticket.
🇮🇩 Indonesia/MoEMR frame for the whole trip: Indonesia's targets are Net Zero Emission by 2060 (or earlier), a 23% renewable share in the national energy mix by 2025 (not met; realization is roughly 13–15%, verify), and the JETP partnership (US$20 billion). The recurring question: which Chinese mechanisms could MoEMR adopt, adapt, or avoid?

TONIGHT (Fri, 9 Oct) → for Sat, 10 Oct

Tomorrow: Ice-breaking, Safety Briefing & Team Building · Lecture 1: China's Basic National Conditions (Zhang Bei) · Opening Ceremony · Welcome Banquet.

A. China context (~45 min)

  • Geography & demography: ~9.6 million km² (similar to Europe), ~1.4 billion people, 56 ethnic groups (Han ~91%). 34 provincial-level divisions: 23 provinces, 5 autonomous regions, 4 municipalities, 2 Special Administrative Regions.
  • Sichuan & Chengdu: Sichuan has ~83 million people; Chengdu (~21 million) is the capital and the southwest hub. Symbols: giant panda, spicy cuisine (麻辣 málà), Dujiangyan.
  • Political system: Communist Party leads; National People's Congress (legislature); State Council (central government). Policy is set through Five-Year Plans (五年规划): the 14th (2021–2025) just ended and the 15th (2026–2030) has begun, the key to understanding energy direction.
  • Economy: second-largest economy, "factory of the world", urbanization ~65%. Rural revitalization (乡村振兴) is a national agenda linked to rural renewables such as biogas.
  • Energy: world's largest energy consumer, still heavily coal-dependent (~55–60% of primary energy), yet also the largest producer of solar, wind, hydropower, batteries and EVs.
  • "Dual Carbon" (双碳) targets: CO₂ peak before 2030, carbon neutrality before 2060; the "1+N" policy framework.
  • Link to Indonesia: major trade and investment partner; Belt and Road / Green Silk Road; nickel, EV battery and solar investments.

B. Ice-breaking & team building (~15 min)

  • Prepare a 60-second introduction: name, institution, duties, one energy problem in your region, one thing you want to learn.
  • Prepare one unique Indonesian fact (e.g., Indonesia holds roughly 40% of the world's geothermal resources, or the household biogas programme).
  • Ask other participants about their country/institution/field and note names for networking.

C. Etiquette, banquet & safety (~15 min)

  • Greetings: "Xièxie" (谢谢, thank you), "Nǐ hǎo" (你好), "Gān bēi" (干杯, cheers; tea or water is fine if you do not drink alcohol).
  • Round-table dining: senior hosts take the seat of honor; never stick chopsticks upright in a bowl; dishes rotate clockwise.
  • Sichuan = hot and numbing (麻辣). Not spicy: "Bú yào là" (不要辣). Halal: "Qīngzhēn" (清真).
  • Note evacuation routes, organizer emergency contacts and field-activity rules.

D. Personal agenda

  • Write 3 personal goals for the 14 days (e.g., "Get a community biogas business model", "Meet 5 experts", "Draft a project proposal for my region").
🇮🇩 Indonesia/MoEMR link:
  • Planning: China steers via Five-Year Plans; Indonesia via RPJMN, the national energy policy (KEN/RUEN) and the electricity plan (RUPTL), the last managed by MoEMR with PLN. Compare how binding each is.
  • Coal dependence: both countries are coal-heavy. Indonesia is a major coal exporter and its power sector is still ~60% coal; MoEMR's task is to balance supply security with coal phase-down (Perpres 112/2022).
💬 Discussion questions:
  • How do Five-Year Plan targets translate into provincial and local implementation, and what happens if a province misses them?
  • Where do rural energy issues sit in China's overall development agenda compared with urban/industrial energy?
  • What did China learn from earlier coal-heavy growth that Southeast Asian countries could avoid?

Saturday night (10 Oct) → for Sun, 11 Oct

Tomorrow: Field Visit 1: Dujiangyan, Water Energy Efficient Utilization & Carbon Neutralization · Cultural Experience 1: Chengdu Giant Panda Garden.

A. Dujiangyan (~45 min)

  • History: built around 256 BCE by Li Bing (李冰) in the Qin era. No dam: nature-aligned engineering still working after 2,200+ years. UNESCO World Heritage (2000) with Mount Qingcheng.
  • Three key components: (1) Yuzui (鱼嘴, "fish mouth") splits the Min River into an inner (irrigation) and an outer (flood) channel; (2) Feishayan (飞沙堰) spillway discharges silt and excess flood water; (3) Baopingkou (宝瓶口, "bottle neck") regulates flow to the Chengdu Plain.
  • Key principles: work with nature, sediment management, flood control + irrigation + transport together. The plain became "Tianfu Zhi Guo" (天府之国, land of abundance).
  • Energy link: Sichuan is China's largest hydropower province (>100 GW incl. Baihetan ~16 GW and Xiluodu ~13.9 GW on the Jinsha River), a base for "West-to-East power transmission" (西电东送).
  • Water-energy-food nexus: a useful lens for the visit.

B. Water-energy efficiency & carbon neutrality (~30 min)

  • Revisit run-of-river vs reservoir hydro, mini/micro hydro (PLTMH for Indonesian villages), hydro capacity factor (~40–50%).
  • Carbon neutrality: sources, sinks (forest, soil, ocean), offsets, carbon markets. China launched its national ETS in 2021 (power sector, later extended to steel, cement, aluminium).

C. Panda Garden (~15 min)

  • IUCN status Vulnerable (downlisted from Endangered in 2016); wild population ~1,800+. Panda conservation illustrates ecological civilization (生态文明), the Monday topic.
  • Indonesia link: two pandas (Cai Tao & Hu Chun) live at Taman Safari Indonesia since 2017.
🇮🇩 Indonesia/MoEMR link:
  • Hydropower: Indonesia's hydro potential is ~95 GW but only a small share is developed (verify). MoEMR promotes both large hydro (e.g., Upper Cisokan pumped storage, Batang Toru, Kayan) and PLTMH/PLTM for remote villages.
  • Carbon pricing: Indonesia has IDXCarbon (launched 2023), the carbon-tax framework and Perpres 98/2021 on carbon economic value; MoEMR runs the power-sector emission trading (PTBAE-PU) pilot. Compare with China's ETS design.
  • Traditional water wisdom: Subak irrigation in Bali (UNESCO 2012) parallels Dujiangyan.
💬 Discussion questions:
  • How is Dujiangyan operated today (digitalization, ecotourism, maintenance funding)?
  • How is water allocated among irrigation, cities and hydropower, and who arbitrates conflicts?
  • What lessons does a dam-free, ecosystem-aligned design offer for Indonesia's run-of-river and micro-hydro planning?
  • How does the ETS price signal actually reach hydro and other clean generators?

Sunday night (11 Oct) → for Mon, 12 Oct

Tomorrow: Lecture 2: Xi Jinping Thought on Ecological Civilization (Wang Qian) · Lecture 3: China's Green Energy Strategy & Policies (Wu Zhenming), both from Sichuan Academy of Social Sciences. The most "policy-heavy" day.

A. Ecological Civilization / 生态文明 (~40 min)

  • Written into the constitution (2018) and national strategy. Goal: "Beautiful China" (美丽中国).
  • Key phrase: "Lucid waters and lush mountains are invaluable assets" (绿水青山就是金山银山): protecting the environment is protecting economic assets.
  • Six core principles (2018 national eco-environmental conference): harmony between humans and nature; green mountains and clear waters as valuable assets; a good environment as the most inclusive public welfare; mountains, waters, forests, farmland, lakes, grasslands and deserts as one living community (山水林田湖草沙); strictest legal system; a shared global community.
  • Instruments: ecological red lines, ecological compensation, carbon markets, central environmental inspections, air and water quality targets.
  • Treat the lecture as policy/ideology: capture the concrete frameworks and instruments.

B. China's green energy strategy & policy (~45 min)

  • Structure: Five-Year Plan → sectoral renewable plans → regulations → markets.
  • Milestones: Renewable Energy Law (2006) (grid purchase obligation, feed-in tariffs); heavy subsidies 2009–2020 drove the PV/wind boom; then transition to subsidy-free grid parity and auctions/markets (2025 pricing reform, "Document 136").
  • Dual Carbon: 2030 targets ~25% non-fossil share and ≥1,200 GW wind + solar (reached early, in 2024). Energy Law in force 2025.
  • 2035 NDC (announced 2025): ~7–10% net emissions cut from peak; non-fossil share >30%; wind + solar ~3,600 GW (verify latest).
  • "Build the new before breaking the old" (先立后破): add clean energy while securing supply; no abrupt coal closure.
  • Rural: village rooftop PV, biogas, clean heating, "rural energy revolution".
🇮🇩 Indonesia/MoEMR link:
  • Comparison table (short): China: Renewable Energy Law 2006 + FiT → auctions/markets. Indonesia: Permen ESDM 50/2017 (later Perpres 112/2022 pricing ceilings), the pending New and Renewable Energy bill (RUU EBET), and PLN's single-buyer structure.
  • MoEMR instruments: KEN/RUEN, NZE roadmap, RUPTL 2025–2034 (greener than predecessors), JETP (US$20 billion), early coal retirement under Perpres 112/2022, the government's 100 GW solar ambition (verify status), and TKDN local-content rules.
  • "Build the new before breaking the old" resembles Indonesia's gradual coal phase-down approach tied to supply security.
💬 Discussion questions:
  • How did China finance this scale of transition, and what were the roles of SOEs versus private firms?
  • What is the biggest barrier now: curtailment, grid, storage, or price signals after the end of subsidies?
  • Which mechanism could Indonesia copy within 3–5 years, and which would not transfer (state-led planning, land, grid ownership)?
  • How are "ecological red lines" enforced when they conflict with energy projects?
  • How did removal of subsidies affect investor confidence, and what does it imply for Indonesia's tariff reform?

Monday night (12 Oct) → for Tue, 13 Oct

Tomorrow: Lecture 4: Development of Rural Renewable Energy Biogas Technology in China (Hu Guoquan) · Lecture 5: Development & Utilization of Biomass Energy in China (He Mingxiong). This is BIOMA's core competence. Study it most deeply.

A. Biogas technology basics (~60 min)

  • Anaerobic digestion (AD), 4 stages: hydrolysis → acidogenesis → acetogenesis → methanogenesis.
  • Composition: CH₄ 50–70%, CO₂ 30–45%, traces of H₂S, NH₃, water vapor. Heating value ~20–25 MJ/m³. Rule of thumb: 1 m³ biogas ≈ 0.6–0.7 m³ natural gas ≈ 1.5–2 kWh electricity (via genset).
  • Operating parameters: temperature (mesophilic ~35 °C, thermophilic ~55 °C), pH 6.8–7.5, C/N ratio ~20–30:1, HRT, OLR, TS/VS, inhibitors (ammonia, H₂S, volatile fatty acids).
  • Digester types: fixed-dome (Chinese model, 水压式沼气池), floating drum (India), tubular/plastic bag (Latin America), CSTR, UASB, plug flow, covered lagoon (POME).
  • Products: biogas (cooking, power, CHP), biomethane (>95% CH₄ via water scrubbing, PSA, membranes, amine), digestate (liquid/solid organic fertilizer).

B. History & models of Chinese biogas (~30 min)

  • Mass rural biogas began in the 1970s, especially in Sichuan; tens of millions of household digesters (~40 million+), the world's largest rural biogas programme.
  • Policy phases: campaign (1970s) → standards & subsidies (2000s) → medium/large & communal plants and biomethane/CNG (2010s onward).
  • Integrated models: "three-in-one" pig–biogas–fruit (south) and "four-in-one" greenhouse–pigsty–biogas–toilet (north).
  • Standards: learn how BIOMA acts as a standards, research, training and testing body.
  • Challenges: operation & maintenance, stable feedstock, unused gas, weak business models, digestate management.

C. Biomass (~30 min)

  • Sources: crop residues (straw, husk, bagasse), forest residues, manure, organic waste, energy crops, used cooking oil.
  • Conversion routes: thermochemical (combustion, gasification, pyrolysis/biochar, torrefaction); biochemical (AD, bioethanol fermentation); physical-chemical (pellets/briquettes, biodiesel, HVO/SAF).
  • China: ~700–800 Mt straw/year, comprehensive utilization >85%; tens of GW biomass power; open straw-burning bans.
🇮🇩 Indonesia/MoEMR link:
  • Biogas: the household biogas programme BIRU (Hivos/SNV with the Indonesian government) since 2009 built tens of thousands of digesters; MoEMR's bioenergy directorate (DJ EBTKE) also supports POME-to-biogas/electricity at palm oil mills. Key lesson to test: why do many small digesters stall?
  • Biodiesel: Indonesia is the world leader in mandatory biodiesel: B35 → B40 (2025) → B50 plan. MoEMR sets the mandate; BPDP funds the price gap.
  • Biomass co-firing in PLN coal plants (MoEMR/PLN programme) and pellet supply chains.
  • Contrast: China's strength is household/communal and agricultural-residue integration; Indonesia's is palm-based liquid biofuels, with weaker rural biogas O&M.
💬 Discussion questions:
  • What are investment cost per m³ digester (household vs communal), lifetime and biogas production cost?
  • What business model and subsidy design prevent digesters from being abandoned? Who funds repairs after year 3?
  • What are the success vs failure rates, and the top three causes of failure?
  • How is straw pretreatment/co-digestion made economical?
  • How is mandatory biofuel/straw-use enforcement handled, and what are the food-vs-fuel and land-use safeguards?

Tuesday night (13 Oct) → for Wed, 14 Oct

Tomorrow (full day 9:00–16:00): Field Visit 2: Jingyang District Biogas Centralized Gas Supply Project, Deyang (Guo Tangping, Deyang Haoyun Composite Materials Co.). Category: "Underdeveloped area".

A. Centralized biogas supply (~50 min)

  • System flow: feedstock collection (manure/straw/waste) → digester → desulfurization & dehydration → storage tank → pipeline network → households (metered) → digestate as fertilizer.
  • Advantages: economies of scale, stable gas quality, professional operators, replaces firewood/LPG. Challenges: pipeline cost, feedstock logistics, affordable tariff, long-term management, safety compliance.
  • Safety: methane is explosive at ~5–15% in air; H₂S is toxic; need gas detectors, flame arrestors, ventilation, emergency procedures.
  • "Composite Materials" in the company name suggests composite/FRP tanks or components. Ask what its role is (investor, operator, technology supplier?).

B. Field observation checklist

  1. Scale: digester volume, biogas capacity (m³/day), households served, pipeline length.
  2. Feedstock: type, origin, transport distance, contracts with farmers.
  3. Gas cleaning: desulfurization, drying, distribution pressure.
  4. Economics: tariff per m³ or per month, O&M cost, other revenue (fertilizer), subsidies, payback.
  5. Institutions: owner (cooperative/village enterprise/company), community involvement, technician training.
  6. Digestate handling and sales.
  7. Safety and monitoring (SCADA, sensors).
  8. Social impact: health, cooking time, cleanliness, farmer income.

C. Practical

  • Field clothing, closed shoes, hat, water, small rain jacket, halal snacks, motion-sickness tablets if needed. Bring a notebook to sketch the process flow diagram on site.
🇮🇩 Indonesia/MoEMR link:
  • Replace LPG: Indonesia imports a large share of LPG and spends heavily on its subsidy; MoEMR pursues LPG substitution (city gas pipelines, DME, induction stoves, biogas). Centralized rural biogas fits this agenda.
  • Institution analogue: BUMDes/cooperatives, the Desa Mandiri Energi concept and MoEMR's rural energy programmes.
  • Target regions: livestock areas in East Java, NTT, Sumatra.
💬 Discussion questions:
  • What is the household tariff versus LPG/firewood cost, and are households willing to pay?
  • Who owns the pipeline and who is responsible when it leaks?
  • What happens to supply in cold months when digester temperature drops?
  • How were farmers persuaded to deliver manure on contract?

Wednesday night (14 Oct) → for Thu, 15 Oct

Tomorrow: Lecture 6: Development & Utilization of Geothermal Energy in China (Prof. Sun Licheng, Sichuan University) · Lecture 7: Hydrogen Energy Development History & Future Outlook (Tang Bo, Chengdu University of Technology).

A. Geothermal (~55 min): Indonesia is a major power here

  • Classification: high temperature (>150 °C, power), medium (90–150 °C), low (<90 °C, direct use/heat pumps). Systems: hydrothermal, EGS, closed-loop/AGS, GSHP, mid-deep borehole heat exchangers.
  • China: world leader in direct use (space heating, spas, agriculture) and heat pumps. Large power plants are limited, e.g., Yangbajing (Tibet) since 1977. Western Sichuan (Kangding, Litang, Batang) has a high-temperature belt; mid-deep geothermal heating in Xiong'an.

B. Hydrogen (~55 min)

  • Colors: grey (reforming), black/brown (coal), blue (+CCS), green (electrolysis + renewables), plus turquoise, yellow, white.
  • Electrolysers: ALK (mature, cheap), PEM (responsive, costly), SOEC (high efficiency, high temperature), AEM (emerging).
  • Value chain: production → compression/liquefaction (350/700 bar; −253 °C) → storage & transport (pipeline, truck, ammonia, LOHC) → use (fuel cells/FCEV, steel and chemicals, storage).
  • China: largest hydrogen producer (~35 Mt/yr, mostly from coal); Hydrogen Industry Medium-Long Term Plan 2021–2035; big green hydrogen projects in Xinjiang and Inner Mongolia; Sichuan/Chengdu uses hydro-based hydrogen.
  • Issues: cost (US$/kg), round-trip efficiency (~30–40%), safety, infrastructure.
🇮🇩 Indonesia/MoEMR link:
  • Geothermal: potential ~23–24 GW (about the second largest in the world) with ~2.6 GW installed, second after the US. Fields: Kamojang, Salak, Darajat, Wayang Windu, Dieng, Sarulla, Lahendong; players: Pertamina Geothermal Energy, Geo Dipa. MoEMR tools: government exploration drilling, risk-mitigation and financing facilities (e.g., the Geothermal Fund via PT SMI), tariff setting and forest-area permitting reforms. Barriers: exploration risk, upfront cost, tariff, permits.
  • Contrast: China uses geothermal mainly for heat; Indonesia almost only for power. Direct-use (agro-drying, tourism, heating) is a largely untapped opportunity.
  • Hydrogen: National Hydrogen Strategy (2023) and Roadmap; PLN and Pertamina pilots (e.g., geothermal-based at Ulubelu); export potential.
💬 Discussion questions:
  • What deep-drilling and EGS technologies has China tested, and at what cost per metre and per kW?
  • How does China de-risk exploration (state drilling, insurance) compared with Indonesia's resource risk mitigation?
  • How could low-temperature geothermal serve agro-processing and tourism in Indonesian villages?
  • When does green hydrogen become competitive, and which sectors first (ammonia, fertilizer, steel, heavy transport)?

Thursday night (15 Oct) → for Fri, 16 Oct

Tomorrow: Field Visit 3: Zhongzi Technology (hydrogen, energy storage, power batteries, new PV materials), HiTech West Zone Chengdu · Field Visit 4: Agricultural Waste Anaerobic Conversion & Green Energy Technology Platform, BIOMA Key Laboratory (Ma Shichun).

A. Energy storage & batteries (~45 min)

  • Why: solar/wind are intermittent, so storage, smart grids and flexibility are needed.
  • Technologies: LFP (safe, cheap, dominant in China), NMC/NCA (high energy density), sodium-ion (emerging), vanadium flow, pumped hydro (dominant by capacity), compressed air, thermal.
  • Terms: kWh/MWh vs kW/MW, C-rate, round-trip efficiency, cycle life, BMS, PCS, EMS. EV batteries: cell → module → pack; second-life and recycling.

B. New PV materials (~20 min)

  • PERC → TOPCon → HJT → perovskite/tandem (lab efficiency 30%+). Monocrystalline silicon dominates; perovskite stability is the challenge.
  • Tip: read the company profile on its official website before visiting and pick 3 products you want to see.

C. Anaerobic conversion at the BIOMA lab (~35 min)

  • Revisit Tuesday's biogas material with focus on agricultural waste.
  • Research concepts: pretreatment (physical, chemical, biological) to break down lignocellulose; co-digestion; BMP test; kinetics; process control and inhibitor removal; biohydrogen; biochar; microbiome (methanogenic archaea).
  • Note equipment and measurement methods on the platform.
🇮🇩 Indonesia/MoEMR link:
  • Batteries: Indonesia's nickel downstreaming (hilirisasi) aims at an EV battery ecosystem; MoEMR supports EV charging infrastructure and the battery-swap/storage agenda. Solar + battery hybrids are central to MoEMR's electrification of 3T (frontier, outermost, least-developed) islands and diesel replacement.
  • Research gap: MoEMR's research agencies (e.g., Lemigas, P3TEK/BRIN partnership) could use BIOMA as a model for joint pilot platforms.
💬 Discussion questions:
  • How does LFP's cost curve change the business case for island microgrids in the tropics (heat, humidity)?
  • What is the methane yield (Nm³/t VS) from different feedstocks, and how does it scale from lab to commercial plant?
  • Could BIOMA train Indonesian technicians/researchers (MoU, joint lab, scholarship)?
  • What are the end-of-life rules for batteries in China, and are they enforced?

Friday night (16 Oct) → for Sat, 17 Oct

Tomorrow: Field Visit 5: Waste-to-Energy Incineration Plant, Xinjin District, Chengdu (Sichuan Hainuoer Eco-Environmental Tech) · Cultural Experience 2: Chengdu Planning Exhibition Hall.

A. Waste-to-Energy incineration (~60 min)

  • Process: waste reception (bunker, mixing) → moving-grate furnace at ≥850 °C with ≥2 s residence time → boiler → turbine & generator → flue-gas treatment → ash.
  • Emission control: SNCR denitrification, semi-dry scrubber, activated carbon injection, bag filter, online CEMS. Focus: dioxins/furans, NOx, SO₂, HCl, heavy metals.
  • Residues: bottom ash (construction material); fly ash = hazardous waste; leachate treatment.
  • Key figures: Chinese MSW calorific value ~5–8 MJ/kg; ~400–600 kWh per tonne; typical plant 500–3,000 t/day.
  • China: world's largest WtE capacity (>1 million t/day), built alongside waste sorting and publicly disclosed online emissions. Social issue: NIMBY opposition and transparency.

B. Chengdu Planning Exhibition Hall (~20 min)

  • "Park City" (公园城市) concept, Tianfu New Area, Chengdu–Chongqing Twin-City Economic Circle, metro and Tianfu airport, low-carbon green city.
🇮🇩 Indonesia/MoEMR link:
  • PSEL (waste-to-electricity): a presidential regulation (Perpres 35/2018, since superseded/updated, verify the current one) mandates acceleration in major cities; MoEMR sets the feed-in price and PLN's purchase obligation. Challenges: wet, unsorted waste, tipping fee, tariff, financing. Compare with RDF and landfill gas.
  • Contrast: China built sorting and strict emissions disclosure first; Indonesia's constraint is the bankability of the tipping fee and tariff.
  • IKN (Nusantara): planning lessons for green-city design and energy infrastructure.
💬 Discussion questions:
  • What are the tipping fee and electricity sales tariff, and who pays the gap?
  • How is sorting enforced and how is dioxin controlled and publicly verified?
  • How were nearby communities consulted, and what compensation was offered?
  • How does Chengdu integrate energy planning into its Park City masterplan?

Sunday night (18 Oct) → for Mon, 19 Oct

Tomorrow: Lecture 8: Development & Status of Solar PV Technology in China · Lecture 9: Development and Status Quo of Wind Power Technology in China (both Huang Ze'ai, Southwest Petroleum University).

A. Solar PV (~55 min)

  • Basics: photovoltaic effect; cell → module → string → inverter → grid. Units: Wp/kWp/MWp, performance ratio, capacity factor (~15–25%), degradation (~0.4–0.5%/yr), DC/AC ratio, MPPT, tracker vs fixed-tilt.
  • Cells: PERC, TOPCon (currently dominant), HJT, perovskite/tandem. Commercial module efficiency ~22–24%.
  • China: >80% of global PV supply chain; installed capacity above ~1,000 GW; steep price falls; overcapacity and "anti-involution" (curbing price wars); curtailment and grid integration; models: utility-scale (desert/plateau), distributed rooftop, agrivoltaics, floating PV.

B. Wind (~45 min)

  • Basics: power ∝ v³; Betz limit 59.3%; power curve; cut-in/rated/cut-out; capacity factor onshore ~25–40%, offshore ~40–50%.
  • Technology: 3-blade HAWT; direct-drive vs gearbox; modern offshore 6–20 MW; monopile/jacket/floating foundations; micro-wind.
  • China: world's largest capacity (~500+ GW), centered in Inner Mongolia, Xinjiang and coasts; major OEMs (Goldwind, Envision, Mingyang). In Sichuan: Liangshan mountains.
🇮🇩 Indonesia/MoEMR link:
  • Solar: huge theoretical potential (thousands of GW); floating PV Cirata (~192 MWp, among the largest in Southeast Asia); rooftop PV rules by ministerial regulation; the 100 GW solar plan (verify); constraints: TKDN, tariffs, grid and remote islands. Opportunity and risk: Chinese modules dominate imports, while Indonesia wants a local manufacturing base.
  • Wind: generally low wind speeds; Sidrap (75 MW) and Jeneponto (72 MW) in South Sulawesi; potential in NTT and South Java. Low-wind-speed turbines and hybrid wind-solar-battery for archipelagic areas.
  • Grid: MoEMR/PLN work on the green super-grid/interconnection concept and storage, comparable to China's West-to-East lines.
💬 Discussion questions:
  • What are PV LCOE (¥/kWh), EPC cost (¥/W) and the village rooftop PV policy?
  • How is China managing overcapacity and curtailment, and what does it mean for module prices Indonesia imports?
  • What module and mounting designs work in hot, humid, corrosive tropical conditions?
  • How does China handle PV module recycling and local-content requirements?
  • Are low-wind-speed turbine designs commercially viable for Indonesia?

Appendix B: Quick Formulas & Figures

  • Wind power: P = ½ · ρ · A · v³ · Cp (Cp ≤ 0.593).
  • Capacity factor: actual annual energy ÷ (capacity × 8,760 h).
  • PV energy: E ≈ kWp × peak sun hours/day × 365 × PR (PR ≈ 0.75–0.85).
  • Biogas → electricity: 1 m³ biogas (CH₄ ~60%) ≈ 1.5–2 kWh (genset efficiency ~30–38%).
  • Methane: heating value ~35.8 MJ/Nm³ (pure); 60% CH₄ biogas ≈ ~21.5 MJ/Nm³.
  • LCOE = (capital cost + lifetime O&M) ÷ (total lifetime energy).
